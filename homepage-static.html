Who
We Are

Seattle is good at helping people live efficiently, but it does not always leave enough time to finish a thought. We created Rainier Literature Society to bring Chinese language, literature, and sustained attention back into one room. We do not define what makes a good reader; we take every honest act of reading and response seriously.

You do not need a polished opinion. Bring a question you are still thinking through.

FIELD NOTES

Someone reads aloud. Someone drifts away. Someone remembers a life from long ago. A photograph keeps one second; the conversation keeps moving.

WHAT WE OFFER

02 / COMMUNITY READING PROGRAMS

What we offer

01

Many Ways to Read

Diverse Reading

We give reading many forms: a play voiced around a table, a poem remade through collage, a shared book opened by a guest who knows its world. You do not need to finish the book or arrive with the right answer. There are many entrances. Curiosity is enough.

02

Experience First

Pure Experience

We are less interested in what anyone should read than in what a page actually does to you. Which line made you stop? Which character unsettled you? A book may return years after the first reading. You do not need to prove that you understood it; you only need to say honestly what you see now.

03

Kindred Spirits

Shared Inquiry

Knowledge here is exchanged, not displayed. One person brings an idea from a book, another brings experience from work, family, or migration, and someone else brings an unfinished question. Good conversation does not rush toward a winner. It lets unfamiliar lives meet for a while.

WHAT'S COMING

03 / UPCOMING GATHERINGS

Coming Up

01 / 01

Chinese

Test Fall Reading

2026-09-20 14:00 · Seattle, WA · Location to be announced

As Seattle begins to cool in September, we want to bring people scattered across the city back to the same table through a reading gathering. The event will begin with a period of quiet reading. Everyone can bring the book they are currently reading—or simply arrive with curiosity. We will then talk about migration, language, memory, and everyday life, sharing the moments when we have come to understand our mother tongue anew in a foreign place. There are no standard answers, and no one is expected to finish a designated book in advance. You can read aloud a favorite passage, tell a story that moved you recently, or simply listen in silence. Paper, pens, tea, and a small selection of snacks will be provided, along with time for informal conversation. We hope this will be more than an event: a slow and sincere encounter that lets strangers connect through words and lets familiar people meet one another again through new questions. Whether you have lived in Seattle for years or have just arrived, you are welcome to join us.
